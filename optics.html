🔍 Optics

Learn reflection, refraction, mirrors, lenses and image formation.

1. Reflection of Light

Reflection is the phenomenon in which light rays bounce back into the same medium after striking a surface.

The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

∠i = ∠r
Ray Diagram — Reflection from a Plane Mirror

2. Refraction of Light

Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes.

n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
Ray Diagram — Refraction from Air to Glass

3. Plane Mirror

A plane mirror has a flat reflecting surface. The image formed by a plane mirror is virtual, erect and of the same size as the object.

Image Formation by Plane Mirror

4. Concave Mirror

A concave mirror has a reflecting surface curved inward. It can form real or virtual images depending on the position of the object.

Concave Mirror — Basic Ray Diagram

5. Convex Mirror

A convex mirror has a reflecting surface curved outward. It always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image.

Convex Mirror — Ray Diagram

6. Convex Lens

A convex lens is thicker at the centre and thinner at the edges. It converges parallel rays of light toward its principal focus.

1/f = 1/v - 1/u
Convex Lens — Converging Rays

7. Concave Lens

A concave lens is thinner at the centre and thicker at the edges. It diverges parallel rays of light.

Concave Lens — Diverging Rays

8. Important Optics Formulas

Mirror Formula:

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Lens Formula:

1/f = 1/v - 1/u

Magnification:

m = hᵢ / hₒ

Refractive Index:

n = c / v

9. Example

A convex lens has focal length 10 cm. An object is placed 20 cm from the lens. Using the lens formula, find the image position.

1/f = 1/v - 1/u

Using the Cartesian sign convention:

f = +10 cm

u = -20 cm

Therefore:

1/10 = 1/v + 1/20

1/v = 1/20

v = +20 cm ✅

📝 Practice Question

A lens has a focal length of 20 cm and an object is placed at -40 cm. Using:

1/f = 1/v - 1/u

Find the image distance v.

Put the values into the lens formula:

1/20 = 1/v - 1/(-40)
