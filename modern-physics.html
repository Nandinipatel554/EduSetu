⚛️ Modern Physics

Learn about photons, photoelectric effect, atoms, radioactivity and nuclear physics.

1. Photoelectric Effect

The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency falls on it.

E = hf

Here, E is photon energy, h is Planck's constant and f is frequency of light.

Photoelectric Effect

2. Photon and Energy

A photon is a small packet of electromagnetic energy. The energy of a photon depends on its frequency.

E = hf

Higher frequency means higher photon energy.

Photon Concept

3. de Broglie Wavelength

According to de Broglie's hypothesis, moving particles also have wave-like properties.

λ = h / p

For a particle of mass m and velocity v:

λ = h / mv
Matter Wave Concept

4. Bohr Model of Atom

In Bohr's model, electrons revolve around the nucleus in specific allowed energy levels.

Electrons can move from one energy level to another by absorbing or emitting energy.

Bohr Model — Energy Levels

5. Radioactivity

Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of radiation from unstable atomic nuclei.

Three common types are:

  • Alpha (α) particles
  • Beta (β) particles
  • Gamma (γ) rays
Radioactive Radiation

6. Nuclear Fission

Nuclear fission is the process in which a heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei and releases a large amount of energy.

Nuclear Fission

7. Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process in which two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus and release a large amount of energy.

Nuclear Fusion

8. Important Formulas

Photon Energy:

E = hf

Energy of Photon using wavelength:

E = hc / λ

de Broglie Wavelength:

λ = h / p

Mass-Energy Relation:

E = mc²

9. Example

Calculate the energy of a photon having frequency 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

Use:

E = hf

Planck's constant:

h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s

Therefore:

E = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 5 × 10¹⁴

E ≈ 3.313 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Using:

E = hf
